# Ordinary transport and custody — Cycle 27

Only explicitly configured technical handcarts and stretchers are operational.
The handcart carries 1–16 ordinary-good or meal units; a stretcher carries one
person and no cargo. These are bounded scenario representations of human-carried
loads, not claims about a sourced historical fleet. Rail remains reference
geometry: operational rail requests are rejected without qualified equipment,
route and service contracts. No inherited special-passenger mechanisms are used.

Each of at most 64 vehicles has a unique regular crew job. Crew must be live,
qualified, on shift, physically present and explicitly authorized to carry;
stretcher crew also need care skill. A finite carried tool is the disclosed
technical equipment requirement. No speed bonus or island movement applies.
Vehicle position follows the actual crew body along reviewed streets and doors.
Starting another trip cannot relocate a vehicle: crew must retrieve its recorded
position. Replacement crew must physically reach it and meet the job/permissions.

Loading/unloading moves one finite unit per shared crew grant. Cargo has exactly
one custody record: source, vehicle, or destination inventory. Food/goods ledgers
include vehicle loads. Routes require qualified links; closures, crowds, weather,
rest, off-shift work, lost equipment and crew loss stop displacement. A stopped
vehicle retains its cargo and actual position. Partially loaded carts can resume
with a qualified co-located replacement. Cancellation unloads through grants at a
physically reached reviewed endpoint; it cannot remotely refund cargo.

Stretcher booking is exclusive. Boarding requires physical co-location and a crew
grant. A carried person retains one native body; that body follows the same
verified crew displacement, with independent movement/work blocked while carried.
Passive carriage does not grant the passenger productive work. Arrival capacity
includes the carried person and incoming connectors; unloading releases custody
at the actual endpoint. A retired passenger is removed safely; crew retirement
leaves the vehicle and remaining custody stationary. Request retries never reload,
recharge or duplicate passengers. New trips use monotonic per-vehicle keys.
