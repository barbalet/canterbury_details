# Ordinary religious employment — Cycle 24

`cd_civic.h` adds distinct offices: priest, minister, rabbi, custodian, sexton,
church/community staff, teacher, administrator and charitable worker. Their base
jobs still use the audited ordinary clergy/lay catalogue; an office does not
rename a Bronze ritual vocation or make every title interchangeable. The generated
`content/religious-worksite-matrix.json` lists eligibility against every supplied
institution, exact denomination/status and candidate premises uncertainty.

Priests require the recorded Anglican/Catholic tradition; ministers require a
Christian institution; rabbis require a Jewish institution. Clerical qualifications
are explicit, adult, institution-and-title-specific scenario inputs. They do not
set personal religious affiliation or membership. Custodians need craft skills;
sextons also need burial permission; teachers need community skills and teaching
permission; staff/administrators need community skills and administration
permission. Charitable workers need community skills. Source staffing, historical
ordination, employment numbers and exact duties remain unknown until supplied.

An office belongs to a regular job attached to the same linked institution and
employer. Ordinary age/skill/reachability/shift/capacity checks still apply. Civic
rosters use separate jobs; a religious title never grants policing, clinical or
fire authority. A vacant or unqualified office produces no scheduled services.
The office records its last qualified holder, successor count and actual service
outputs. Losing staff pauses requests; a qualified replacement can resume the
remaining work, while prior wages stay earned. Institution closure also gates
ordinary productive work attached to an office. Custodial service uses the existing
qualified lay-work recipe; structural repair/capacity works retain Cycle 22 rules.

No source institution is auto-staffed. The actual map still needs reviewed routes,
entrances and capacities. Operational acceptance uses explicitly labeled technical
geometry and staff fixtures. All titles describe ordinary employment, with no
supernatural effects or inferred moral/personality traits.
